# Chapter 11 — Two‑Stage Eschatological Prophecy

(These are independent interpretive reflections and do not represent the consensus of mainstream churches. Interpretive sources include Zechariah, Ezekiel, Isaiah, Revelation, 1 Corinthians, 1 Thessalonians and 2 Peter. This chapter builds upon the prior framework of judgment and the two Books of Life. It contrasts two eschatological timelines: prophecies with a millennial intermediate stage, and narratives that move straight to the eternal kingdom without a millennium.) ^PFP-00314

## 1. Recap of Previous Chapters

We have confirmed that Zechariah and the Book of Revelation share a consistent prophetic style, in which prophecies fulfilled in two distinct stages are often presented side by side. For example, the prophecy in Zechariah 9:9 was fulfilled at the Messiah’s first coming, while the prophecy in Zechariah 9:10 awaits fulfillment in the eschatological age. Revelation 11:2–3 likewise contains prophecies with two separate stages of fulfillment. This chapter seeks to demonstrate that the two-stage eschatological framework laid out in Revelation 20 and 21 — the Millennial kingdom and the eternal new heavens and new earth — aligns with the prophecies of the Old Testament prophets. ^PFP-00315

## 2. Dual‑Layer Eschatological Prophecy in Isaiah: Distinguishing the Millennial Kingdom and the Eternal New Creation

Many prophecies of peace among animals and restored environment in Isaiah must not be indiscriminately assigned to the new heaven and new earth. The classic passage is Isaiah 11:6–9: The wolf will live with the lamb, the leopard will lie down with the goat, the calf and the lion and the yearling together; and a little child will lead them. The cow will feed with the bear, their young will lie down together, and the lion will eat grass like the ox. The infant will play near the cobra’s den, and the young child will put its hand into the viper’s nest. They will neither harm nor destroy on all my holy mountain, for the earth will be filled with the knowledge of Yehowah as the waters cover the sea. ^PFP-00316

This scene describes the millennial kingdom, not the final new heaven and new earth. Serpents, lions and livestock are still present. The order of the present created world is modified: the natures of fierce beasts are changed so they no longer injure. Nevertheless, the fundamental structure of the created material order has not yet been fully renewed. ^PFP-00317

Isaiah delivers a higher promise later in Isaiah 65:17–25, which also contains two layers: ^PFP-00318

Beginning at 65:17: “See, I will create new heavens and a new earth. The former things will not be remembered, nor will they come to mind.” This marks the start of the eternal kingdom after the millennium, when the old creation has passed away entirely. ^PFP-00319

Verses 65:20–25 still speak of children dying at a hundred years old, people building houses, planting vineyards and bearing children. Harm from enemies is restrained. This depicts conditions on the earth during the millennium. ^PFP-00320

The prophet received one revelation and set the earthly millennial kingdom side by side with the eternal new creation that follows it. The two visions must be separated in sequence and not conflated. The millennium is a temporary, peaceful reign upon the earth; the new heaven and new earth is the everlasting state where the old creation is abolished, death and corruption exist no more. ^PFP-00321

## 3. Two Eschatological Passages in Paul: 1 Corinthians 15 and 1 Thessalonians 4 contain no millennial stage

### 3.1 1 Corinthians 15:23–28, 50–54

> [!scripture]
> But each in turn: Messiah, the firstfruits; then, when he comes, those who belong to Messiah. Then the end will come, when he hands over the kingdom to God the Father after he has destroyed all dominion, authority and power. For he must reign until he has put all his enemies under his feet. The last enemy to be destroyed is death… When he has put everything under his feet, then the Son himself will be made subject to him who put everything under him, so that God may be all in all.

^PFP-00322

> [!scripture]
> I declare to you, brothers and sisters, that flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God, nor does the perishable inherit the imperishable. Listen, I tell you a mystery: We will not all sleep, but we will all be changed — in a flash, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed. The perishable must clothe itself with the imperishable, and the mortal with immortality. When the perishable has been clothed with the imperishable, and the mortal with immortality, then the saying that is written will come true: “Death has been swallowed up in victory.

^PFP-00323

Paul sets out the order of resurrection: Messiah is the firstfruits; at his coming, those who belong to Messiah are raised. Immediately after the resurrection comes the end. Messiah subdues all hostile powers, delivers the kingdom to the Father, and death, the final enemy, is destroyed. ^PFP-00324

Within this narrative, there is no intermediate period of a thousand years of earthly reign following Messiah’s return and the resurrection of the saints. Once transformed, the righteous enter directly into the imperishable eternal kingdom where God is all in all. ^PFP-00325

### 3.2 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17 (the rapture passage)

> [!scripture]
> For the Lord himself will come down from heaven, with a loud command, with the voice of the archangel and with the trumpet call of God, and the dead in Messiah will rise first. After that, we who are still alive and are left will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air. And so we will be with the Lord forever.

^PFP-00326

According to Paul’s account, two events unfold simultaneously at Messiah’s appearing: the dead saints rise first, and the living saints are immediately caught up to meet the Lord in the air. They then enter unbroken fellowship with Him forever. There is no delay leading into an earthly millennial kingdom. ^PFP-00327

Paul’s eschatological framework is consistent across these texts: Messiah’s arrival, resurrection and transformation of the saints, the rapture to meet the Lord, the arrival of the end, the overthrow of all enemies, and entry into the eternal kingdom. ^PFP-00328

## 4. The Resurrection Sequence in Revelation 20:4–6 for Comparison

Revelation presents a different sequence of resurrection: Following the victory at Armageddon, the martyred saints take part in the first resurrection. They are raised and reign with Messiah on the earth for a thousand years. After the millennium ends, the rest of the dead are raised and stand before the Great White Throne for the final judgment, the second resurrection. ^PFP-00329

The core contrast: ^PFP-00330

Paul (1 Cor 15, 1 Thess 4): Messiah’s coming → resurrection and transformation of those in Messiah, caught up to meet the Lord in the air → the end arrives, all enemies are subdued, entry into eternity. No earthly millennial reign. ^PFP-00331

Revelation (Revelation 20): Victory at Armageddon → first resurrection of martyred saints → millennial reign on earth with Messiah; after the millennium, the rest of the dead are raised for final judgment. ^PFP-00332

## 5. Connection with the Broader Interpretive System

Isaiah and the Revelation of John mutually confirm this two‑vision pattern of eschatology: the millennium (earthly, limited in duration, still containing death, childbearing and sacrifices), followed by the new heaven and new earth (eternal, no more death). ^PFP-00333

Paul’s teaching on the order of resurrection and the end in 1 Corinthians 15, together with the rapture account in 1 Thessalonians 4, describes a single, final consummation with no intervening millennial earthly reign. 2 Peter follows the same pattern: at the day of the Lord, the heavens and earth are dissolved and the new creation arrives directly. This creates a clear contrast: the Old Testament prophets and John’s Revelation supply a staged end‑time timeline; Paul’s epistles and 2 Peter present a single‑endpoint timeline. These two cannot be harmonised on a plain‑literal chronological reading. ^PFP-00334

## 6. Supplementary Note

Many mainstream interpreters force the rapture text of 1 Thessalonians 4 and the resurrection passage of 1 Corinthians 15 into the millennial timeline of Revelation, developing pre‑trib, mid‑trib and post‑trib rapture schemes. Yet on a plain reading of the narrative: Revelation plainly places the first resurrection before the millennium, for those raised to reign on the earth. Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 15 that resurrection leads straight to the end, death swallowed up, and entry into the imperishable kingdom. 1 Thessalonians 4 describes resurrection and the rapture as meeting the Lord in the air, to be with Him forever. Neither passage contains a description of a thousand‑year earthly reign. This distinction is inherent to the two frameworks. ^PFP-00335
