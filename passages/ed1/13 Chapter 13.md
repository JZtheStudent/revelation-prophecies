# Chapter 13 — The Erroneous Foundation of “Justification by Faith”

(These are independent interpretive reflections and do not represent mainstream church consensus. This chapter follows the two‑layer structure of redemption from the previous chapter. It examines deviations in the traditional doctrine of “justification by faith” from the Hebrew original text, Greek verb tenses, narrative context and apostolic qualification, and distinguishes the biblical truth of justification by faithfulness from the later theological framework.) ^PFP-00348

## 1. The Original Meaning of Genesis and Habakkuk: Textual Bias in Paul’s Theological Foundation

Paul built his system of “justification by faith” mainly on two passages: Genesis 15:6 and Habakkuk 2:4. In the Hebrew original, the syntax, lexical meaning and narrative intent of these verses differ fundamentally from the interpretation Paul adopted from the Septuagint. The doctrinal conflicts and textual tensions of the past two thousand years all stem from this shift in translation and interpretation. ^PFP-00349

Genesis 15:6 follows strict Hebrew syntax of waw‑consecutive narrative. When no new subject is introduced in a linked clause, the subject continues from the preceding sentence. The prior subject is Abram, so the proper reading is: Abram believed in Yehowah, and Abram acknowledged Yehowah as righteous and faithful. This is an Old‑Testament confession, praise and covenant affirmation. It is not the reading reshaped by the Septuagint: “God reckoned Abram’s faith to him as righteousness.” ^PFP-00350

The Old‑Testament tradition of prayer and confession confirms this meaning. Nehemiah 9 records the Israelites confessing and praising God’s righteousness and faithfulness in repentance. The core Hebrew understanding is this: God’s righteousness consists in keeping covenant and fulfilling promises. When a man receives God’s promise by faith, he acknowledges that the God of the covenant is righteous and trustworthy. His faith is not a token that can be exchanged and counted as his own righteousness. ^PFP-00351

The key term in Habakkuk 2:4, אֱמוּנָה (ʾemunah), consistently means steady faithfulness, covenant‑keeping and abiding loyalty throughout the Hebrew Bible. It is not a one‑off, momentary act of believing. The verse contrasts the proud with the righteous: the righteous live by an enduring posture of abiding in truth and loyalty to God, not by a single isolated act of faith. ^PFP-00352

The Septuagint made a decisive interpretive alteration: it shifted the subject in Genesis 15:6 and rendered the Hebrew “faithfulness” in Habakkuk as the Greek πίστις, understood as a single act of belief. Paul adopted this Septuagint reading and built the whole framework: a man is reckoned righteous by one act of faith, apart from works of the law. ^PFP-00353

## 2. The Corrective of James: Rejecting Artificial Harmonization and Returning to the Unified Biblical Standard

To reconcile Paul and James, mainstream theology invented the two‑kind justification theory: Paul speaks of positional justification, while James speaks of justification demonstrated by works. This distinction is not found in Scripture. It is a later device invented to preserve Paul’s teaching and patch over textual contradiction. ^PFP-00354

Read in its original context, James is fully consistent with the Hebrew Scriptures. James 2 quotes Genesis 15:6 and states plainly that genuine faith is accompanied by obedient works and is brought to completion through works. Abram’s confession was not a static, one‑time belief; it was a living faithfulness that issued in obedience, such as offering Isaac. ^PFP-00355

James also establishes the unity of the law: whoever breaks one commandment is guilty of the whole law. The righteous standard of the law has never been lowered or annulled. Any transgression, whether great or small, deliberate or accidental, is lawlessness and sin. ^PFP-00356

At the same time, James reveals the full dimension of grace: mercy triumphs over judgment. God’s righteous judgment remains unchanging, yet He has prepared mercy for the repentant. This is the core thread running through the Old Testament, James and John: the law is perfect; men stumble in weakness. But repentance, confession and mercy bring forgiveness and justification before God. ^PFP-00357

## 3. Precise Greek Tense in 1 John: Habitual Sin versus Occasional Failure

Through careful Greek tense usage, 1 John clarifies the two biblical definitions of sin, harmonises with James and the Old Testament, and undermines the foundation of Paul’s system. ^PFP-00358

1 John 3: Habitual, persistent lawlessness (sin as a settled way of life) Verses 3:4–9 use the customary present tense, which corresponds to “keep on sinning” in translations such as the NIV. It describes a continuing pattern of sin, a lifestyle of lawlessness without repentance. This does not mean believers must never stumble even once in this life. Its meaning is: those born of God do not have a settled identity in ongoing lawlessness. Whoever continues habitually in sin has not known God and does not belong to Him. This is the test of true and false believers: not whether one has stumbled once, but whether one’s settled life‑pattern is persistent lawlessness. ^PFP-00359

1 John 1:9: Occasional, accidental failure (the believer’s weakness and fall) 1:9 uses a present conditional combined with aorist aspect, designed for isolated, unexpected falls of believers. ^PFP-00360

- The present conditional points to repeated occasions of confession throughout life;
- The aorist means each time a person confesses, God cleanses that specific transgression completely and immediately.

^PFP-00361

Verse 1:8 adds: all men have occasional failures. If anyone claims he has no sin, he deceives himself. ^PFP-00362

This yields a consistent, complete biblical framework of sin and righteousness: ^PFP-00363

- The law is a perfect standard; breaking one commandment is breaking the whole law.
- The unregenerate live in habitual lawlessness and are bound by sin.
- The regenerate live in settled obedience, with only occasional weaknesses forgiven through confession and mercy.
- Mercy is tied to human forgiveness: the measure by which you forgive others is the measure by which God forgives you.

^PFP-00364

## 4. The Unified Standard of the Righteous in the Old Testament: No One Is Sinless; All Are Justified by Repentance and Mercy

The Old Testament confirms the witness of James and John: the righteous in Scripture are never people who never stumble. Abraham, David, Moses and Job all had clear weaknesses and failures. They were called righteous for three reasons: ^PFP-00365

- Their settled life‑orientation was trust and obedience toward God;
- When they fell, they repented and humbled themselves;
- They relied on God’s mercy and covenant grace, not on their own sinlessness.

^PFP-00366

God says He dwells among the congregation of the righteous. The Old‑Testament definition runs unbroken: The righteous = one whose settled way is obedience, who repents when he falls, and who trusts in God’s mercy. This is not what Paul’s system teaches: that man cannot keep the law at all, that any failure disqualifies him from justification by the law, and that the way of law is entirely closed. ^PFP-00367

## 5. Fundamental Conflict between Paul’s Theology and the Main Line of Scripture

Building on the biased Septuagint reading, Paul developed a separate system incompatible with the unified witness of Scripture. ^PFP-00368

Paul’s core logic All men have sinned and fall short of God’s glory; no one can keep the whole law. Any transgression makes justification by the law impossible. Therefore man must abandon works of the law and be reckoned righteous by faith alone. ^PFP-00369

The unified logic of the Old Testament, James and John The law is the eternal perfect standard of righteousness and remains unannulled. A man may stumble occasionally and still stand under the law’s judgment. Yet repentance, confession, mercy and grace bring forgiveness and justification. The test of true and false believers is whether they persist in habitual lawlessness. ^PFP-00370

The two accounts cannot be reconciled: Paul: failure makes justification by the law impossible; the law’s standard must be set aside, and righteousness comes only by faith. The unified biblical witness: failure may be forgiven through repentance and mercy; the law’s standard abides, and works prove genuine faith. ^PFP-00371

Paul’s system of “faith apart from works, righteousness reckoned by a single act of faith” rests on mistranslation and misinterpretation, not the original revelation of God. ^PFP-00372

## 6. A Chain of Scriptural Evidence: Paul as a False Apostle

The biblical qualifications for a true apostle The fixed standard for apostles in Acts and 1 John is: ^PFP-00373

- Followed Yeshua from baptism to ascension;
- Saw and handled the risen Lord;
- Belonged to the twelve foundational apostles replacing Judas. Paul never satisfied any of these criteria, and he acknowledged he was not one of the twelve.

^PFP-00374

The twelve foundations of the New Jerusalem Revelation 21:14 states clearly that the twelve foundations of the city bear the names of the Lamb’s twelve apostles. The eternal foundational apostles of the church are the twelve personally chosen by Yeshua. Paul is not among them, which proves he does not hold the office of a true apostle. ^PFP-00375

Greek tense in 2 Timothy 1:15: the churches of Asia turned away from Paul as a body The main verb ἀπεστράφησαν (turned away) is aorist: ^PFP-00376

- It marks a single, decisive, settled corporate judgment;
- It is not temporary fear or casual withdrawal in persecution;
- Timothy knew of this as a settled fact, not a sudden surprise. The churches of Asia, including Ephesus, made a collective, definitive decision to reject Paul. This was not mere personal estrangement; it was a verdict after testing, rejecting his apostleship and teaching.

^PFP-00377

Revelation 2:2: the Ephesian church tested and rejected false apostles The Lord commends Ephesus: they tested those who claimed to be apostles and found them false. The text speaks of false apostles in the plural, matching the background of many self‑styled apostles in the early church. Paul was the central figure among them. God did not name him at that time because of the timetable of eschatological prophecy: If exposed immediately, the two‑thousand‑year system of false apostles and the beast‑system of the papal power could not unfold, and the prophecies of the end could not be fulfilled. God left clues hidden, for later believers to assemble and test the truth. ^PFP-00378

## 7. Conclusion: The Boundary between the Unified Biblical Truth and Later Deviation

### The unified biblical truth (Old Testament, James, John):

- The law is the eternal standard of righteousness; breaking one commandment is breaking the whole law.
- No human being is sinless in this life. The righteous are those whose settled way is obedience, who repent when they fall, and who trust in God’s mercy.
- Genuine faith is accompanied by obedience; faith without works is dead.
- Occasional transgression is forgiven through confession and the grace of mercy.
- Whoever persists in habitual lawlessness has never belonged to God.

^PFP-00379

### Paul’s deviant theological system (built on the Septuagint misreading):

- Because no man can keep the law perfectly, justification by the law is ruled out entirely.
- A single act of faith is isolated as the sole condition for justification, severing faith from works.
- It forms a special doctrinal system at odds with the main witness of Scripture, requiring artificial harmonisation.

^PFP-00380

### Final verdict on apostleship

- Only the twelve are the eternal foundational apostles of the church.
- Paul fails the biblical tests for apostleship. The collective rejection by the churches of Asia is the very event of discerning false apostles confirmed by the Lord in Revelation.

^PFP-00381

There is no contradiction in Scripture. All tensions arise from later mistranslation, misinterpretation, and the implantation of Paul’s deviant theological system. Only by returning to the Hebrew original and the Greek tense‑sense can we recover the original, pure, unified truth of God’s revelation. ^PFP-00382
