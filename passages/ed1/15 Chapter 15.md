# Chapter 15 — The Redemption of Yeshua at the Cross

(These are independent interpretive reflections and do not represent the consensus of mainstream churches. Interpretive sources include Isaiah, Exodus, the Gospel of John, 1 John and the Sermon on the Mount in Matthew. This chapter follows the prior framework of eschatology and the order of the law. It unpacks the two‑layered structure of cross redemption and corrects the mainstream reading of “once saved, always saved”.) ^PFP-00391

The full biblical definition of the redemption accomplished by Yeshua at the cross has been severely simplified, distorted, and even reversed by later mainstream theology. Mainstream churches interpret the work of the cross in this way: by one crucifixion, Yeshua fully fulfilled the entire law on behalf of mankind, secured final salvation for humanity, and whoever believes once is eternally secure, free from judgment under the law and can never perish. ^PFP-00392

This system of “once saved, always saved; grace removes responsibility under the law” conflicts with the Old Testament prophecies, the Passover type, Yeshua’s own words, and the whole redemptive structure of Scripture. Only by returning to the Messianic prophecy of Isaiah 53 and the original type of the Passover Lamb, together with the witness of 1 John, can we recover the genuine meaning of the redemption of the cross. ^PFP-00393

## 1. Isaiah 53: The Redemptive Mission of the Suffering Servant — Bearing Iniquity and Justifying Many

The central Old Testament prophecy of redemption defines the work of Yeshua on the cross. ^PFP-00394

Isaiah 53 foretells clearly: The righteous Servant was pierced for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities; Yehowah laid on Him the iniquity of us all; He poured out His life unto death, bore the sin of many, and justified many. ^PFP-00395

This foundational prophecy is the bedrock of New Testament truth concerning redemption. The prophet states plainly: the Messiah’s death is a substitutionary bearing of human sin, bringing forgiveness and justification. ^PFP-00396

Yet Isaiah as a whole, and the entire Old Testament, never defines this redemption as “once and for all final, never to fail afterwards, requiring no continuing obedience from man”. His work removes the penalty of sin, restores the covenant relationship between man and God, and grants man the qualification and opportunity to follow and obey Yehowah — it does not cancel the lifelong responsibility to walk according to the law. ^PFP-00397

## 2. New Testament Confirmation: Yeshua is the Lamb of God Who Takes Away the Sin of the World

John the Baptist fulfils Isaiah’s prophecy and declares concerning Yeshua: “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world.” (John 1:29) ^PFP-00398

At the Last Supper, Yeshua formally unites His death with the Old Testament Passover: “This is my body, which is given for you; this is the blood of my new covenant, poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins.” ^PFP-00399

Scripture thus settles the framework: Yeshua is the ultimate Passover Lamb. The blood of the cross corresponds to the blood of the Passover lamb in the Old Testament. ^PFP-00400

## 3. The Ultimate Type of the Old Testament Passover: Uncovering the Greatest Misconception of Later Redemptive Theology

The Passover in Exodus was established by Yehowah Himself as a complete foreshadowing of the New Testament redemption of the cross, without ambiguity. ^PFP-00401

The destroying angel passed over the Israelite households in Egypt not because the Israelites were perfect or sinless, but because: the blood of the lamb was applied to the doorposts. The blood stood in place of the death of the firstborn, and judgment passed over them. ^PFP-00402

This is the first layer of truth in the type: the lamb’s blood forgives former sin, removes the penalty of judgment, and delivers from death. ^PFP-00403

This is the first, foundational effect of the cross: ^PFP-00404

- To blot out and forgive all past sins
- To deliver from the judgment that brings death
- To redeem from the world, darkness and the authority of death
- To bring man into a new covenant relationship with God

^PFP-00405

The great interpretive error of mainstream churches is to cut off the second half of the type: they fixate only on “salvation at Passover” and ignore the final outcome of the forty years in the wilderness. ^PFP-00406

## 4. Passover Redemption is Salvation at the Starting Point, Not Entrance into the Inheritance at the End

All Israelites experienced the Passover, all had blood on their doorposts, all escaped the destroying angel, all came out of Egypt, all were saved by God. ^PFP-00407

By the modern mainstream logic of “once saved, always saved”: if all were saved, all should enter the Promised Land, receive the inheritance and attain full victory. ^PFP-00408

But the biblical record is the opposite: of the millions who came out of Egypt, nearly all died in the wilderness. Only Joshua and Caleb, and the next generation, finally entered the land of promise. ^PFP-00409

This thoroughly refutes the core error of mainstream theology: Passover redemption is a starting‑point salvation, bringing passage from death into life. It is not final, ultimate salvation that guarantees entry into the kingdom. ^PFP-00410

Scripture reveals a consistent two‑layer redemptive structure, uniting Old and New Testaments: Layer One: Passover Redemption (the starting point at the cross) ^PFP-00411

- The blood atones for past sins
- Deliverance from judgment and death
- Regeneration, belonging to the Lord, entrance into the covenant
- Receiving the Holy Spirit and new life
- Becoming God’s saved people, children of God — this does not automatically grant entry into the kingdom and the inheritance

^PFP-00412

1 John 3:2 confirms this: “Beloved, now we are children of God; and it has not yet been revealed what we shall be, but we know that when He is revealed, we shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is.” ^PFP-00413

The verse clearly distinguishes present identity from final destiny. Believers already possess the status of children of God by the grace of the cross, yet the final form and end are not yet fully revealed. That final outcome is bound to the direction of one’s life on earth, continuing obedience, and turning away from lawlessness. Being a child of God is the beginning, not an unconditional guarantee. Growing and being conformed to Messiah is the path toward final glory. ^PFP-00414

Verse 8 of the same chapter lays bare the nature of this spiritual warfare: “He who sins is of the devil, for the devil has sinned from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil.” ^PFP-00415

The devil’s ongoing work is to tempt men to sin. Even when believers do not wish to sin, the devil keeps tempting and ensnaring them in transgression. The incarnation, cross work and gift of the Holy Spirit are directed against the devil’s works. The effect of Messiah’s work is not merely forgiveness of past sin; it also supplies power so believers may no longer live habitually under sin’s dominion, and may resist sin and walk in obedience to the law. ^PFP-00416

### Layer Two: Lifelong Obedience in the Wilderness (the end of sanctification)

- Walking in the wilderness of this life, keeping God’s law continually
- Continual repentance, faithfulness and submission to the Father’s will
- Overcoming temptation, rebellion and lawlessness, resisting the devil’s temptation to sin
- Finally entering the eternal Promised Land and receiving the inheritance of the kingdom

^PFP-00417

All Israelites experienced the first layer of redemption, the exodus from Egypt. Yet most failed in the wilderness journey of the second layer. Through rebellion, unbelief and transgression of God’s law, they followed the devil’s temptation and sinned, and perished in the wilderness without entering the inheritance. ^PFP-00418

This fully explains the whole teaching of Yeshua’s Sermon on the Mount: many are redeemed, call Him Lord, work miracles, preach and cast out demons, yet are rejected by Him in the end because of lawlessness. ^PFP-00419

## 5. The True Function of Cross Grace: Power to Keep the Law, Not Abolish the Law

Together with Chapter 8 on Yeshua’s teaching of the law, we arrive at a complete, non‑contradictory truth of redemption: ^PFP-00420

In the flesh, man cannot keep the law perfectly, cannot overcome sin, and cannot remain faithful. The devil tempts unceasingly, and man has no strength to resist in himself. ^PFP-00421

Therefore God prepared the redemption of the cross and the grace of the Holy Spirit: ^PFP-00422

Grace does not release man from keeping the law; grace gives man the power to keep the law, breaking the devil’s authority to draw men into sin. ^PFP-00423

- The cross forgives all our past failures and transgressions.
- Regeneration establishes a new foundation of life, making us children of God, the present identity described in 1 John 3:2.
- The indwelling Holy Spirit supplies inward strength to overcome the flesh, weakness, the devil’s temptation, and to walk according to the law, fulfilling 1 John 3:8: the Son of God appeared to destroy the devil’s works of sin.

^PFP-00424

This is the true meaning of Yeshua’s statement “you must be born again”. Regeneration does not mean sin no longer counts as sin for you; it means you are delivered from the old life, in which the flesh is weak and the devil easily captures you to sin, into a new life empowered by the Spirit to walk in obedience to God’s law. ^PFP-00425

## 6. Exposing the Fatal Distortion of the Cross in Mainstream Churches

Influenced by a skewed interpretation of Paul in later generations, mainstream churches have altered the full redemption of the cross. ^PFP-00426

## 7. Conclusion: The Ultimate Placement of the Work of the Cross

The prophecy of Isaiah 53, the Lamb‑centred redemption of the New Testament, the Passover type, and the witness of 1 John 3:2 & 3:8 confirm one another: ^PFP-00427

The cross of Yeshua is the starting point of redemption, not the end. ^PFP-00428

The blood deals with past sin, the penalty of judgment and the authority of death. The appearing of Messiah and the gift of the Spirit are to destroy the devil’s works of sin, grant us power over sin, inaugurate a new life turned toward God, and make us children of God now. ^PFP-00429

As 1 John 3:2 warns, although we are children of God now, what we shall be has not yet been revealed. The true kingdom inheritance and final salvation rest upon: regenerate starting point + lifelong obedience to God’s law + continual turning away from lawlessness + resisting the devil’s temptation by the Spirit + submission to the Father’s will. ^PFP-00430

Mainstream theology twists the “starting grace and present sonship” into an end‑state licence and unconditional guarantee. It sets aside responsibility under the law, ignores the reality of the devil’s ongoing temptation, and misleads countless believers to live in lawlessness while supposing they are eternally saved, so they end up rejected by the Lord as described in Matthew 7. ^PFP-00431

The ultimate meaning of the cross is not to release man from keeping the law. It is that by the blood we receive forgiveness, by the Spirit we receive power, we break the devil’s power to cause sin, and we truly fulfil, exalt and obey God’s eternal law — fully fulfilling Isaiah 42:21 and the whole teaching of the Sermon on the Mount. ^PFP-00432
