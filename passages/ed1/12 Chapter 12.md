# Chapter 12 — The Millennial Order

(These are independent interpretive reflections and do not represent the consensus of mainstream churches. Interpretive sources include Genesis, the Gospel of Luke, Galatians, Romans, Ezekiel and Zechariah. This chapter follows the two‑stage eschatological prophecy framework of the previous chapter. It contrasts the everlasting covenants of the Old Testament, the teachings of Yeshua and Paul’s arguments, and examines the ritual order of the millennial temple.) ^PFP-00336

A common practice in mainstream interpretation is to reconcile Paul’s words in Galatians with the everlasting covenants of the Old Testament. Interpreters seek to explain Paul’s position merely as opposition to justification by circumcision, rather than a prohibition of physical circumcision itself. Such reconciliation, however, lacks scriptural support. In Galatians, Paul states plainly and directly: if anyone receives physical circumcision, they fall from grace and are cut off from Messiah. This is not a later misreading of Paul; it is his explicit instruction, in which he tells believers not to receive physical circumcision. ^PFP-00337

Genesis 17 establishes circumcision as the everlasting covenant between Yehowah and Abraham. “Everlasting” means this covenant established by God cannot be annulled unilaterally by any human being. Yeshua was circumcised on the eighth day according to the law (Luke 2:21). In all four Gospels, there is no record of Yeshua uttering a single word declaring that the everlasting covenant of circumcision is abolished. Yeshua came to fulfil the law of Moses. The law He proclaimed is one and the same with the law of Moses; He did not establish a separate new law distinct from the Mosaic law. ^PFP-00338

Paul, nevertheless, coined a unique term: the law of Messiah. This expression does not appear in the Gospels and was not spoken by Yeshua. Yeshua did not inaugurate a separate legal system. This “law of Messiah” was constructed by Paul and put forward under the name of Messiah. In Romans 3:31 Paul claims: “Do we, then, nullify the law by this faith? Not at all! Rather, we uphold the law.” The law Paul says he “upholds” here is not the Mosaic law, but his own defined “law of Messiah”. If he referred to the Mosaic law, this would create an irresolvable conflict with his prohibition of physical circumcision in Galatians and his discussion of the Mosaic law in 2 Corinthians 3. With this statement “we uphold the law”, he creates the impression that he still affirms the Mosaic law as a self‑defensive argument; this is a plausible‑but‑false teaching. In Galatians, Paul further narrows the core of the law to one single command: “love your neighbour as yourself”. In doing so, he weakens and sets aside the first and greatest commandment of the law: to love Yehowah with all one’s heart. This stands in clear contrast with Yeshua’s own words: Yeshua explicitly states that the first commandment is love for God, and only after that, love for one’s neighbour. ^PFP-00339

The millennial temple vision in Ezekiel 40–48 confirms that the everlasting covenant of circumcision has never been revoked by Yehowah. Ezekiel 44:9 states: “This is what the Sovereign Lord says: No foreigner uncircumcised in heart and flesh is to enter my sanctuary.” The text places heart circumcision and physical circumcision side by side; both are required for entry into the sanctuary, and neither can replace the other. If Paul’s prohibition of physical circumcision were a command from Yeshua, an official ordinance of God, then in the millennial reign of the Messiah, Yehowah would not still require physical circumcision as a prerequisite for entering the sanctuary. Zechariah 14:16–19 prophesies that all nations will go up to Jerusalem each year to celebrate the Festival of Tabernacles in the millennium. The Zechariah passage only records the universal observance of Tabernacles and does not mention a sacrificial system at the millennial temple. ^PFP-00340

Many interpreters cite Romans 2:28–29, “a person is a Jew inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart”, to soften Paul’s stance and argue that Paul only emphasises inner reality. Yet this passage is still Paul’s own formulation. It only proves that physical circumcision without inner repentance is worthless. It does not prove that God has abolished the everlasting covenant of physical circumcision made with Abraham. ^PFP-00341

A clear distinction emerges here: ^PFP-00342

Established by God, upheld and never revoked by Yeshua: the everlasting covenant of Abrahamic circumcision and the full Mosaic law. Its foremost commandment is love for God, followed by love for one’s neighbour. Within the millennial temple order, Yehowah still requires both heart and physical circumcision, and commands all nations to keep the Festival of Tabernacles. ^PFP-00343

Teachings advanced by Paul: he originates the term “law of Messiah”; in Romans 3:31 he claims to “uphold the law” as self‑defence, though the law he upholds is not the Mosaic law but his own law of Messiah. In Galatians he states plainly that anyone who receives physical circumcision falls from grace. He reduces the law to the single maxim “love your neighbour as yourself”, omitting the first commandment to love God. This teaching was not delivered by Yeshua and conflicts with Yeshua’s words and the everlasting covenants of the Old Testament. ^PFP-00344

Ezekiel 40–48 also records sacrifices, priestly statutes and purification rules for the millennial temple. Messiah’s once‑for‑all sacrifice on the cross is the sole foundation for atonement. Millennial sacrifices are not offered for the remission of sin; they are ordained by Yehowah’s sovereign will as memorial ordinances and rites of sanctification. This likewise demonstrates that Messiah’s completed redemption does not mean all ceremonial ordinances and covenant signs are abolished. ^PFP-00345

Summary: Paul’s warning in Galatians is not a later misreading; it is his direct instruction for believers not to receive physical circumcision. No human being has authority to annul the everlasting covenant between Yehowah and Abraham, and Yeshua never abolished circumcision. Paul’s claim in Romans that he does not nullify the law but upholds it is self‑defence. The law he refers to is his self‑invented “law of Messiah”, not the Mosaic law. The “law of Messiah” was not proclaimed by Yeshua. Paul contracts the law to “love your neighbour as yourself”, removing the primary commandment to love God. This diverges from Yeshua’s teaching and the full law of the Old Testament. Ezekiel’s millennial vision reveals that in God’s future plan, physical circumcision remains necessary for sanctuary entry, and heart circumcision cannot by itself replace the physical covenant sign. ^PFP-00346

Note: Genesis 17:13 establishes circumcision as an everlasting covenant. Yeshua was circumcised in Luke 2:21, and no Gospel text records Yeshua abolishing circumcision. The Hebrew original of Ezekiel 44:9 places uncircumcision of the heart and uncircumcision of the flesh together as twin barriers to sanctuary entry. The term “law of Messiah” appears only in Paul’s epistles and nowhere in Yeshua’s teaching in the Gospels. Zechariah 14:16–19 only foretells the nations keeping Tabernacles and does not describe millennial sacrifices. Yeshua identifies the greatest commandment as love for God, followed by love for neighbour; Galatians focuses only on love for neighbour. Romans 3:31, the claim that the law is upheld, is often used to create the impression that Paul does not oppose the Mosaic law, while in reality the law he advocates is his own law of Messiah, which stands in tension with the Mosaic law. ^PFP-00347
