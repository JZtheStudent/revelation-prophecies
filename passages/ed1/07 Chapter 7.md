# Chapter 7 — The Root Cause of Babylon’s Judgment: Scriptural and Prophetic Proof That the Trinity Is a Man-Made Heresy

The ultimate judgment upon Babylon the Great, the harlot riding upon the beast as described in the Book of Revelation, springs not merely from worldly corruption, idolatry or persecution of the saints. Its central and fatal spiritual transgression lies in its imposition across the whole earth of a divinely authoritative doctrinal system nowhere plainly revealed in Scripture, constructed centuries later by human reasoning: the doctrine of the Trinity. This man-made theology was set up as the universal standard of orthodox faith, binding the nations, marking dissenters as heretics, and claiming spiritual dominion. It constitutes the foundational blasphemy for which Babylon incurs God’s fiery judgment. ^PFP-00255

This spiritual apostasy was foretold with precision in Daniel chapter 7. In Daniel’s vision of the fourth beast among the ten horns, a blasphemous boastful little horn arose, and three of the former horns were plucked up by the roots before it (Daniel 7:8). History bears its witness: these three overthrown kingdoms were the Heruli, the Vandals, and the Ostrogoths, the three major Arian kingdoms. These three nations shared one core conviction: they firmly rejected the Trinity and upheld the truth that the Father alone is the Most High, while the Son holds a subordinate status as the One sent by the Father. The little horn that rose to dominate them stands for the Papacy of Rome. ^PFP-00256

To enthrone the Trinity as official orthodoxy and secure Rome’s unified theological supremacy, the Papacy deployed political and military force to eradicate these three anti-Trinitarian kingdoms in AD 493, 534 and 538. It uprooted opposing faith traditions, erased records, and monopolized scriptural interpretation, ushering in the era when the Trinity became the binding creed of the institutional church. ^PFP-00257

Daniel’s prophecy confirms that the arrogant power enforcing the Trinity is the very authority detested and marked for overthrow by God. By contrast, the three horns violently uprooted by this power represented communities preserving the original biblical monotheistic truth. It follows that the Trinitarian system is not a revelation from God, but a man-made heresy abominable in His sight. It is the defining mark of blasphemy belonging to Babylon the Great, and therefore it is destined for God’s final judgment. ^PFP-00258

The term “Trinity” and its complete doctrinal formulation do not appear anywhere in the canon of Scripture. The teaching that the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit are three co-equal persons of one identical essence was formally codified by the church between the second and fourth centuries AD, and cannot be used as a presupposition when interpreting biblical texts. In the personal teachings recorded in the Gospels, Yeshua never claimed to share identical essence with the Father. He consistently called God His Father and stated plainly, “the Father is greater than I” (John 14:28). Even in the Book of Revelation, the exalted Messiah still addresses the Most High as His God and His Father; the Son remains perpetually in the subordinate position of a Son under the Father. ^PFP-00259

The Old Testament employs Hebrew substitutive rhetoric in its accounts of the Angel of Yahweh. In the narrative of the pillar of cloud and pillar of fire leading Israel out of Egypt, the same event is sometimes attributed to Yahweh and sometimes to the Angel of Yahweh. This Hebrew literary convention means that the acts performed by an authorized representative are credited to the One who sent him. As John 1:18 declares, no man has seen the Father. Every Old Testament appearance described as a manifestation of Yahweh comes through this unique heavenly representative, the Angel of Yahweh. Worship rendered to this angel is directed toward Yahweh who sent him, not to the angel in himself. The capitalization of “Angel of the Lord” in the King James Version is merely an editorial choice; the original Hebrew text had no upper and lower case letters. ^PFP-00260

The word often translated “begotten” is a covenantal symbolic term, not a literal description of divine begetting. The Hebrew verb yalad in Psalm 2:7 belongs to the royal coronation idiom of the ancient Near East: when a king was installed under covenant, he was declared a son, receiving authority and status, not being literally born. The Greek word monogenes in John’s writings fundamentally means unique, one of a kind; it carries no inherent sense of being begotten. The KJV rendering “only begotten” is the choice of English translators. “The only begotten Son” in John 1:18, “the Only Son who is in the bosom of the Father and declares Him”, is a symbolic title granted by the Father to His exclusive Representative, not a description of a second divine person begotten within eternity. ^PFP-00261

A critical distinction of the Greek definite article appears in John 1:1. The God in “the Word was with God” carries the definite article and refers specifically to the Father, the Most High God. The θεός in the final clause “the Word was God” lacks the definite article. Ancient Greek had no indefinite article equivalent to English a/an. If John intended to state plainly that the Word was identical to the Father, the Most High God, he could easily have added the definite article. His deliberate omission opens multiple interpretive possibilities. Many English versions uniformly read “The Word was God”, smoothing away this grammatical distinction; this is a translation choice shaped by theological presupposition, not a grammatically mandatory rendering. Colwell’s Rule only describes a Greek linguistic pattern and cannot conclusively prove that this anarthrous θεός must refer to the Father. This verse may be understood to mean that the Word is the Father’s authorized representative endowed with divine authority, distinct from the Father’s own essence. ^PFP-00262

In John 10:34, Yeshua cites Psalm 82:6, where earthly judges are called “gods” because they received judicial commission from Yahweh. Yeshua’s reasoning demonstrates that an authorized agent may bear the title “god” without being the Most High Father Himself. “Son of God” is this supreme title conferred upon the Messiah, the Representative sent by God. ^PFP-00263

When Yeshua says “I and my Father are one” (John 10:30), the same Greek word for “one” recurs in John 17:21, where Yeshua prays that the disciples “may all be one”. The disciples cannot merge into one God. This proves the “oneness” here refers to unity of will, spirit and purpose, not identical essence. The Trinitarian claim of consubstantiality is an inference imposed onto the text, not its plain sense. ^PFP-00264

Philippians chapter 2 is one of the chief passages later Trinitarian theologians drew upon to build and defend their doctrine. The text tells how Yeshua was exalted to the highest place and given the name above every name. Trinitarian interpreters use this passage to argue that Christ is equal with the Father. Paul’s epistle became foundational material for later Trinitarian thought, yet such readings cannot be found in the direct personal teaching of Yeshua in the Gospels. ^PFP-00265

Revelation 22:8–9 sets forth Scripture’s clear principle of worship. John fell down to worship the angel (who is Yeshua Himself), and the angel immediately forbade him, commanding that only God is to be worshipped. As a created servant, the angel rejects human adoration. The practice of worshipping Yeshua in mainstream Trinitarian churches rests upon the later Trinitarian theological framework rather than the plain teaching of Scripture. ^PFP-00266

Taken together, Scripture presents a consistent pattern: Yeshua is the Father’s unique heavenly Representative, invested with supreme delegated authority and divine titles, yet forever subordinate to the Father. The Trinity is a theological system constructed centuries after the biblical canon was completed. It was largely developed from interpretations of Pauline texts such as Philippians chapter 2, rather than from the plain reading of Yeshua’s own words. The prophetic judgment foretold in Daniel and the final judgment of Babylon the Great in Revelation jointly confirm: the system enforcing the doctrine of the Trinity is the harlot and antichrist power the Bible predicts God will utterly destroy. ^PFP-00267
